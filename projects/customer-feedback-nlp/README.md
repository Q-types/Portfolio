# Customer-feedback NLP

**Completed Cambridge coursework, with a new offline evaluation on invented feedback.** My contribution was review preparation, comparison of topic representations, pretrained emotion inference and a separate local issue-extraction prompt experiment. The aim was to identify themes worth investigating while keeping customer statements distinct from model-generated interpretations.

I compared **BERTopic and LDA** for topic analysis. These describe groups of documents: embeddings and clustering in the former, word distributions in the latter. I used a **pretrained BERT classifier** to infer emotions, without fine-tuning it. A separate **local Phi-4-mini** experiment explored issue extraction on bounded samples. The final notebook code supports these roles; it does not establish full-corpus extraction accuracy or business improvement.

A useful extraction needs more than fluent wording. It must retain what happened, avoid unsupported detail, distinguish uncertainty and preserve multiple issues without counting repeats twice. Topic coherence and readable themes help assess representations, but neither measures whether an individual extracted claim is true to a review.

The [new runnable example](../../examples/customer_feedback_nlp/README.md) makes those distinctions inspectable. A Codex agent independently invented a small corpus, reference issue rubric, mock extractions and semantic annotations before measurement. No original text or outputs were adapted. There was no independent human adjudication and no new language-model call. Deterministic code verifies review IDs, exact evidence spans and hashes, then accounts for the authored judgments using one-to-one issue matching.

The retained mock results include **three unsupported claims out of eleven**, one ambiguous claim and one repeated supported issue. Six of nine definite reference issues are matched, leaving three omissions. One of the six unique matches has the wrong category and one lacks useful specificity. The negative-rated no-issue control also exposes an invented refund claim. These are intentional, retained failure cases in an authored example, not measured model performance.

An actual, bounded **scikit-learn LDA** comparison also ran offline on the ten eligible invented reviews, with two and three topics. Both configurations and their document weights are retained. In-sample perplexity describes fit to these words; it does not score issue extraction, select a business solution or establish generalisation. BERTopic, pretrained emotion inference and Phi-4 were not rerun.

Reviews are self-selected observations. Language and rating filters, empty text, source mix and location coverage all affect the denominator. In the original notebooks, source-specific language handling and ordered prefix sampling further limit comparability. The new example keeps every eligible negative review, including uncertain and no-issue cases, and reports source/location counts rather than presenting location rankings.
