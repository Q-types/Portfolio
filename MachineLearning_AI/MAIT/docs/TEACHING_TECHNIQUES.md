# Teaching Techniques & Pedagogical Framework

## 📚 Overview

This document explains the evidence-based teaching techniques embedded in MAIT's lesson planning system. All techniques are derived from PGCE (Postgraduate Certificate in Education) training and cognitive science research.

---

## 🎯 1. Curriculum Planning Techniques

### 1.1 Backward Design

**Definition**: Planning lessons by starting with the end goals and working backwards.

**Process**:
1. Identify desired learning outcomes
2. Determine acceptable evidence of learning
3. Plan instructional activities to achieve outcomes

**Why It Works**: Ensures every activity has a clear purpose and connects to assessment criteria.

**In MAIT**: Every lesson starts with clear, measurable objectives aligned to AQA specifications.

**Evidence Base**: Wiggins & McTighe (2005) - "Understanding by Design"

---

### 1.2 Curriculum Mapping

**Definition**: Creating a visual representation showing how content connects across topics and time.

**Key Features**:
- Identifies core skills and content
- Shows progression and connections
- Ensures comprehensive coverage
- Prevents gaps and unnecessary repetition

**In MAIT**: Lessons reference specific AQA chapter codes and prerequisites.

---

### 1.3 Interleaving and Spacing

**Definition**: 
- **Interleaving**: Mixing different topics or types of problems
- **Spacing**: Distributing practice over time rather than cramming

**Why It Works**: 
- Strengthens memory consolidation
- Improves long-term retention (spacing effect)
- Enhances ability to discriminate between concepts

**In MAIT**: Recall sections revisit previous topics; practice activities mix question types.

**Evidence Base**: Rohrer & Taylor (2007) - Cognitive Psychology

---

### 1.4 Knowledge Organizers

**Definition**: One-page summaries showing essential knowledge, vocabulary, and connections for a topic.

**Components**:
- Key vocabulary with definitions
- Core concepts and their relationships
- Visual representations (diagrams, flowcharts)
- Essential facts students must memorize

**In MAIT**: Lesson summaries distill key takeaways in an organized format.

---

### 1.5 Sequencing Learning

**Definition**: Arranging content in a logical, progressive order that builds on prior knowledge.

**Principles**:
- Simple before complex
- Concrete before abstract
- Known before unknown
- Part before whole (when appropriate)

**In MAIT**: Lessons follow a structured sequence from recall → new knowledge → application.

## 🎓 2. Explaining and Modelling Techniques

### 2.1 Dual Coding

**Definition**: Using both verbal and visual information simultaneously to enhance learning.

**Why It Works**: 
- Information encoded in multiple formats is more memorable
- Appeals to different learning preferences
- Reduces cognitive load by distributing processing across channels

**Application**:
- Diagrams alongside text explanations
- Charts with verbal descriptions
- Student-created visual representations

**In MAIT**: Generated content includes suggestions for diagrams, models, and visual aids.

**Evidence Base**: Paivio (1971, 1986) - Dual Coding Theory

---

### 2.2 Worked Examples

**Definition**: Step-by-step demonstrations showing how to complete a task or solve a problem.

**Structure**:
1. State the problem
2. Show each step explicitly
3. Explain the reasoning at each stage
4. Highlight key decision points

**Why It Works**: 
- Reduces cognitive load for novices
- Makes expert thinking visible
- Provides a model for students to follow

**Fading Strategy**: Gradually remove support as students gain competence.

**In MAIT**: "New Knowledge" sections include explicit modeling and examples.

**Evidence Base**: Sweller & Cooper (1985) - Cognitive Load Theory

---

### 2.3 I-We-You Method (Gradual Release of Responsibility)

**Definition**: Three-stage approach transferring responsibility from teacher to student.

**Stages**:
1. **I (Modeling)**: Teacher demonstrates while thinking aloud
2. **We (Guided Practice)**: Teacher and students work together
3. **You (Independent Practice)**: Students work independently

**Why It Works**: 
- Scaffolds learning appropriately
- Builds confidence gradually
- Allows for formative assessment at each stage

**In MAIT**: Lesson structure moves from teacher-led (New Knowledge) to student-led (Apply to Demonstrate).

**Evidence Base**: Pearson & Gallagher (1983)

---

### 2.4 Cognitive Load Management

**Definition**: Strategies to optimize working memory usage during learning.

**Types of Cognitive Load**:
- **Intrinsic**: Inherent complexity of the content
- **Extraneous**: Unnecessary load from poor instruction
- **Germane**: Productive load that builds schemas

**Strategies**:
- Break complex information into chunks
- Remove unnecessary information
- Use clear, concise instructions
- Provide scaffolding and support

**In MAIT**: Content is broken into distinct sections; differentiated tasks manage complexity.

**Evidence Base**: Sweller (1988) - Cognitive Load Theory

---

### 2.5 Conceptual Modelling

**Definition**: Creating abstract representations (diagrams, flowcharts, models) to explain complex ideas.

**Types**:
- Concept maps (showing relationships)
- Flowcharts (showing processes)
- Physical models (representing structures)
- Analogies and metaphors

**In MAIT**: "Connection to Real World" uses models and analogies to make concepts accessible.

## 💬 3. Questioning and Feedback Techniques

### 3.1 Cold Calling

**Definition**: Asking questions to students regardless of whether hands are raised.

**Benefits**:
- Keeps all students engaged
- Reduces "opt-out" mentality
- Provides formative assessment data
- Creates a culture of participation

**Best Practices**:
- Supportive, not punitive
- Give think time
- Follow up with probing questions
- Frame as low-stakes opportunities

**In MAIT**: Lessons include questions suitable for cold calling at key points.

---

### 3.2 Socratic Questioning

**Definition**: Using probing questions to encourage critical thinking and examine assumptions.

**Question Types**:
1. **Clarification**: "What do you mean by...?"
2. **Assumptions**: "What are we assuming here?"
3. **Evidence**: "How do you know? What evidence supports that?"
4. **Viewpoints**: "What would someone who disagrees say?"
5. **Implications**: "What would happen if...?"
6. **Meta-questions**: "Why is this question important?"

**In MAIT**: Discussion questions use Socratic techniques to deepen understanding.

---

### 3.3 Think-Pair-Share

**Definition**: Collaborative strategy where students think individually, discuss with a partner, then share with the class.

**Process**:
1. **Think** (30-60 seconds): Individual reflection
2. **Pair** (1-2 minutes): Discuss with partner
3. **Share** (varying): Selected pairs share with class

**Benefits**:
- Increases participation
- Provides rehearsal time
- Builds confidence
- Encourages peer learning

**In MAIT**: Activities often suggest think-pair-share as an engagement strategy.

---

### 3.4 Effective Questioning Techniques

**Bloom's Taxonomy Question Levels**:
1. **Remembering**: Define, list, identify
2. **Understanding**: Explain, describe, summarize
3. **Applying**: Use, demonstrate, solve
4. **Analyzing**: Compare, contrast, examine
5. **Evaluating**: Judge, justify, critique
6. **Creating**: Design, construct, develop

**Best Practices**:
- Use a variety of question types
- Provide adequate wait time (3-5 seconds)
- Use follow-up and probing questions
- Check for understanding with all students

**In MAIT**: Questions and Answers section includes varied question levels.

---

### 3.5 Feedback Loops

**Definition**: Cycles of performance → feedback → improvement.

**Effective Feedback**:
- **Timely**: Given soon after performance
- **Specific**: Precise, not vague
- **Actionable**: Student knows what to do next
- **Kind**: Supportive tone
- **Focused**: On learning goals, not just compliance

**Types**:
- Teacher feedback
- Self-assessment
- Peer feedback
- Automated feedback (quizzes, etc.)

**In MAIT**: Assessment opportunities built into multiple lesson stages.

**Evidence Base**: Hattie & Timperley (2007) - "The Power of Feedback"

## 🔄 4. Practice and Retrieval Techniques

### 4.1 Retrieval Practice

**Definition**: Actively recalling information from memory to strengthen learning.

**Why It Works**:
- Testing effect: retrieval strengthens memory more than re-studying
- Identifies gaps in knowledge
- Improves long-term retention

**Methods**:
- Low-stakes quizzes
- Flashcards
- Brain dumps
- Practice questions

**In MAIT**: Recall/Starter section activates prior knowledge through retrieval.

**Evidence Base**: Roediger & Butler (2011)

---

### 4.2 Spacing and Interleaving

**Spacing**: Distributing practice over time
**Interleaving**: Mixing different types of problems or topics

**Optimal Spacing**:
- Review after 1 day
- Review after 1 week
- Review after 1 month
- Review before assessment

**In MAIT**: Recall sections revisit previous content; questions mix topics.

**Evidence Base**: Cepeda et al. (2006) - Distributed Practice

---

### 4.3 Deliberate Practice

**Definition**: Focused practice aimed at improving specific aspects of performance.

**Components**:
1. **Specific goals**: Clear target for improvement
2. **Focused attention**: Concentration on task
3. **Immediate feedback**: Know if you're correct
4. **Reflection**: Think about performance
5. **Repetition**: Practice until automatic

**In MAIT**: "Apply to Demonstrate" provides structured practice opportunities.

**Evidence Base**: Ericsson, Krampe, & Tesch-Römer (1993)

---

### 4.4 Quizzing and Testing

**Definition**: Using tests as learning tools, not just assessments.

**Benefits**:
- Testing effect (retrieval practice)
- Identifies knowledge gaps
- Reduces test anxiety
- Provides feedback

**Best Practices**:
- Frequent, low-stakes quizzes
- Immediate feedback
- Opportunities to learn from mistakes
- Mix of question types

**In MAIT**: Multiple assessment opportunities throughout lessons.

---

### 4.5 Distributed Practice

**Definition**: Spreading study sessions over time rather than massing practice.

**Why It Works**:
- Allows time for memory consolidation
- Prevents fatigue and cognitive overload
- Leads to better long-term retention

**In MAIT**: Lessons designed to be part of a sequence, not standalone.

## 🎭 5. Mode B Teaching (Student-Centered Approaches)

### 5.1 Flipped Classroom

**Definition**: Students learn content at home (via videos, readings) and do "homework" (application, practice) in class.

**Benefits**:
- More class time for high-value activities
- Students learn at their own pace
- Teacher available for complex tasks

**In MAIT**: Content can be adapted for flipped format with video resources.

---

### 5.2 Project-Based Learning

**Definition**: Students learn by engaging in real-world, meaningful projects.

**Key Features**:
- Driving question or problem
- Extended timeline
- Student voice and choice
- Authentic audience
- Reflection

**In MAIT**: "Connection to Real World" provides project ideas.

---

### 5.3 Independent Study

**Definition**: Self-directed learning where students pursue topics of interest.

**Teacher Role**:
- Provide resources and guidance
- Set checkpoints and timelines
- Support goal-setting
- Facilitate reflection

**In MAIT**: Extension tasks for high attainers encourage independent research.

---

### 5.4 Peer Teaching

**Definition**: Students teaching each other, reinforcing their own understanding.

**Approaches**:
- Reciprocal teaching
- Peer tutoring
- Jigsaw method
- Think-pair-share

**Benefits**:
- Deepens understanding (to teach is to learn twice)
- Builds communication skills
- Increases engagement

**In MAIT**: Activities often suggest collaborative work.

---

### 5.5 Inquiry-Based Learning

**Definition**: Learning through questioning, investigating, and constructing understanding.

**Process**:
1. Pose a question
2. Formulate hypotheses
3. Investigate
4. Analyze results
5. Draw conclusions
6. Communicate findings

**In MAIT**: Science lessons naturally incorporate scientific inquiry.

---

## 🛠️ 6. Scaffolding Techniques

### 6.1 Breaking Down Tasks

**Definition**: Dividing complex tasks into smaller, manageable steps.

**Application**:
- Step-by-step instructions
- Checklists
- Success criteria
- Exemplars

**In MAIT**: Differentiated tasks break down complexity for lower attainers.

---

### 6.2 Providing Hints

**Definition**: Offering clues or suggestions without giving away answers.

**Types**:
- Directive hints (what to do next)
- Supportive hints (how to think about it)
- Resources (where to find information)

---

### 6.3 Visual Aids

**Definition**: Using charts, diagrams, and visual tools to support understanding.

**Types**:
- Graphic organizers
- Concept maps
- Diagrams and illustrations
- Anchor charts

**In MAIT**: Dual coding approach incorporates visual support.

---

### 6.4 Feedback and Encouragement

**Definition**: Providing constructive feedback and motivational support.

**Growth Mindset Language**:
- "Not yet" instead of "can't"
- Praise effort and strategy, not just intelligence
- Frame mistakes as learning opportunities

**In MAIT**: Task instructions include supportive language.

---

## 🎯 7. Differentiation Strategies

### 7.1 Varied Instructional Approaches

**Definition**: Using multiple teaching methods to reach all learners.

**Approaches**:
- Direct instruction
- Discovery learning
- Cooperative learning
- Independent study

**In MAIT**: Four attainment levels receive different instructional approaches.

---

### 7.2 Multiple Means of Engagement

**Definition**: Providing various ways for students to engage with material.

**Strategies**:
- Choice of topics or tasks
- Varied difficulty levels
- Different roles in group work
- Connections to interests

---

### 7.3 Different Modes of Assessment

**Definition**: Allowing students to demonstrate understanding in multiple ways.

**Options**:
- Written assessments
- Oral presentations
- Projects and portfolios
- Performances

**In MAIT**: Differentiated tasks include varied assessment modes.

---

### 7.4 Flexible Grouping

**Definition**: Grouping students flexibly based on needs, not fixed ability groups.

**Grouping Options**:
- Homogeneous (similar ability)
- Heterogeneous (mixed ability)
- Interest-based
- Random
- Student choice

**In MAIT**: Activities suggest appropriate grouping strategies.

## 🎯 Application in MAIT

Every MAIT-generated lesson incorporates multiple techniques:

1. **Backward Design**: Objectives drive content
2. **Retrieval Practice**: Recall/Starter activates memory
3. **Worked Examples**: Modeling in New Knowledge
4. **Dual Coding**: Visual + verbal representations
5. **Differentiation**: Four attainment levels
6. **Formative Assessment**: Questions throughout

This comprehensive approach ensures every lesson is pedagogically sound, evidence-based, and effective for diverse learners.
